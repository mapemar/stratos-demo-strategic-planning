# SACE Historical Financial Intelligence — 2021–2025
**Research cut-off:** 15 November 2026  
**Primary perimeter:** SACE S.p.A. standalone. Group consolidated figures are not mixed into the historical series.

## Executive findings

1. **A five-year comparison is possible, but not as a simple like-for-like growth series.** The operating model and accounting perimeter changed materially through MEF risk-sharing, emergency guarantee schemes, the transition away from COVID-era operations and the introduction of Archimede.
2. **Premiums alone are misleading.** Gross premiums fell from €403.9m in 2023 to €221.5m in 2024, but SACE explicitly attributes this largely to the MEF co-insurance structure: 78% of total premiums did not pass through SACE's own P&L in 2024. For Export and strategic relevance the split is 90% SACE / 10% MEF; for Archimede 80% SACE / 20% MEF.
3. **Underwriting profit is affected by reserves and recoveries, not only current-year defaults.** The net claims charge is the net accounting movement after claims, recoveries and claims reserves. It is not equivalent to cash claims paid or the ultimate loss on the year's new business.
4. **2023–24 profits require normalisation.** 2023 included €122.9m relating to the release/unwind of MEF-related technical-reserve costs for 2020–2023. SACE also distributed €780m to its shareholder in 2023 between ordinary dividend and available reserves. The 2024 result benefited from investment income and positive technical performance.
5. **Investment income is a material earnings driver.** The financial management result was €351m in 2024 and €249.6m in 2025. SACE attributes the 2025 decline partly to the prior year's inverted yield curve and higher returns on liquidity, while the investment strategy is liability-driven.
6. **Claims and recovery cash flows can be lumpy.** In 2024 large settlements in infrastructure/construction, gas and aeronautical sectors drove very high gross claims paid, while recovery settlements materially reduced net claims expense. 2025 gross claims paid fell substantially and recovery inflows included large items from Ireland, Iraq and Saudi Arabia.

## Key institutional and accounting discontinuities

### 2020–2021: emergency response and MEF risk transfer
The 2021 accounts describe the transfer/reinsurance to MEF of up to 90% of the performing portfolio at 8 April 2020, with a corresponding MEF payable recognised in 2020 and partly repaid in 2021. Garanzia Italia, commercial-credit support and Green guarantees were recorded in separate managements. From 1 January 2021 the SACE–MEF co-insurance framework for Export & Internationalisation became operational. In 2021 SACE mobilised €27.6bn: €12.7bn export/internationalisation, €12.0bn Garanzia Italia and €2.9bn Green New Deal.

### 2022: ownership transfer and energy shock
From March 2022 SACE was directly controlled by MEF following the transfer of the group (excluding SIMEST) from CDP. SupportItalia was added in response to the energy-price and liquidity shock. The MEF annual report records €44.169bn of total resources mobilised in 2022, including €10.945bn Garanzia Italia, €18.016bn SupportItalia, €12.215bn Export & Strategic Relevance and €3.020bn Green New Deal.

### 2023: legacy unwind and exceptional profit movements
The Court of Audit's 2023 report describes the shift from a company retaining much of its own risks to one transferring a large share of risks to MEF. The report records the 2023 result of the technical account at €309.8m and net profit at €398.2m. It notes the €122.9m related to the 2020–2023 MEF-reinsured portfolio reserve unwind/cost reimbursement. SACE's June 2024 release states that €780m was distributed to MEF between ordinary dividend and available reserves.

### 2024: Archimede starts and co-insurance accounting becomes visible
Archimede guarantees can have a maximum duration of 25 years. For each commitment, SACE assumes 20% and the State 80%, without joint liability. 2024 perfected Archimede guarantees were €4.697bn against a €10bn annual ceiling. SACE reports that 78% of total premiums did not pass through its P&L due to the MEF co-insurance structure. The portfolio managed including the MEF share was €146.1bn at year-end 2024.

### 2025: mix shifts towards Archimede and domestic strategic relevance
Gross premiums were €282.5m, of which €138m were new-production premiums. Archimede gross premiums totalled €266.9m, of which approximately €213.5m related to the MEF share; SACE's own P&L recorded €53.4m for Archimede. SACE's managed portfolio exposure on its balance-sheet perimeter was €51.3bn, with €39.4bn private, €11.6bn sovereign and €0.36bn political risk. Project-finance exposure fell 19.4% to €7.65bn while corporate-credit exposure rose 7.6% to €12.71bn. Investment management result was €249.6m vs €351.0m in 2024.

## What this means for STRATOS financial modelling

The current three-year model should not be calibrated by applying a single loss ratio to aggregate guarantees. It should be rebuilt around five linked ledgers:

1. **Commercial production and portfolio:** approvals, perfected commitments, utilised exposure, amortisation/run-off, tenor, currency, sector, country, buyer, product and risk bearer.
2. **Risk allocation:** SACE own-account share, MEF/state-account share, reinsurance share, treaty limits, collateral and counterparty exposure. Separate legal co-insurance from reinsurance.
3. **Insurance technical account:** gross and ceded premiums, unearned premium reserve, expected-loss / premium reserve, claims incurred, claims paid, case reserves, IBNR where relevant, recoveries and their timing, reinsurance recoverables and commissions.
4. **Investment and ALM:** asset classes, book/fair value, duration, yield, reinvestment, spread and FX risk, realised/unrealised P&L, liquidity buffers and liability cash-flow matching.
5. **Capital and shareholder flows:** own funds/equity, subordinated funding, technical reserves, dividend and reserve distributions, capital consumption, state guarantee/fund capacity and risk appetite.

### Additional model variables to introduce

- PD / default frequency by obligor rating, country, sector and product.
- LGD and recovery rate by collateral, insolvency regime, sovereign restructuring and time-to-recovery.
- EAD / utilisation, tenor, amortisation, cancellation and prepayment.
- Claim notification-to-payment lag; claim severity and dispute / waiting periods.
- Reserve adequacy and lifetime expected-loss assumptions; discount rate and forward-looking overlays.
- MEF/state share and fund coverage, separate from SACE own-account exposure.
- Reinsurance pricing, capacity, counterparty default and recoverable collection delay.
- Interest-rate curve level, slope and reinvestment rates; credit spreads and realised gains/losses.
- FX movements, sanctions, convertibility and transfer restrictions.
- Operating cost inflation, claims handling costs, acquisition costs and business mix.
- Dividends, extraordinary distributions, capital injections and retained earnings.
- Concentration by country, sector, buyer, bank and correlated supply chain.

## Priority reconciliations before using forecasts

- Reconcile gross premiums to total premiums including MEF share, especially Archimede.
- Reconcile portfolio managed including MEF share to SACE balance-sheet exposure and SACE retained risk.
- Reconcile gross claims paid, reinsurer/MEF shares, recoveries, net claims incurred and claims reserves.
- Separate recurring technical/financial earnings from one-off reserve releases, extraordinary items and shareholder distributions.
- Keep SACE standalone, SACE Group consolidated, SACE BT, SACE FCT and state-account managed operations distinct.
